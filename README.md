# Bengaluru Climate Risk Scanner

A Web GIS application that tells a bank how much of its property loan book sits in **flood** and **extreme-heat** danger zones in Bengaluru, and checks its flood map against the real **September 2022 Bengaluru floods**.

A user uploads a CSV of property locations in the browser. Each property gets a flood score, a heat score and an overall risk level (Low, Medium, High). A dashboard summarises the portfolio, including the headline metric that the Reserve Bank of India's draft climate disclosure framework (February 2024) asks lenders to report: **the share of assets exposed to climate risk**.

## Why it matters

On 4 to 5 September 2022, overflowing lakes and stormwater drains flooded large parts of Bengaluru's IT corridor (Outer Ring Road, Bellandur, Sarjapur Road, Varthur). Because Bengaluru's floods follow its chain of lakes and drains, the risk has a clear spatial pattern that GIS can model and test.

## Tech stack

| Layer | Tools |
| --- | --- |
| Spatial analysis | Python (geopandas, rasterio, osmnx, numpy), Google Earth Engine, Google Colab |
| Publishing | GeoServer: WMS, WFS, WFS-T; SLD styles; GML |
| Web app | Leaflet, HTML/CSS/JavaScript, Chart.js, PapaParse; talks to GeoServer only through WMS, WFS (XML filters, GML) and WFS-T |
| Hosting | XAMPP Apache with two small PHP files: a read-only forwarder to GeoServer (same origin, no CORS) and a guard for WFS-T writes |

## Running Notebook 01

1. Open `data-prep/01_data_download.ipynb` in Google Colab (File → Open notebook → GitHub).
2. In the config cell, set `ee_project` to your own Google Earth Engine Cloud project.
3. Run all cells. Outputs are saved to `MyDrive/bengaluru-climate-risk/` in Google Drive.

Data files (`.gpkg`, `.tif`) are not stored in this repository. They are regenerated by the notebooks.

## Data sources

| Data | Source |
| --- | --- |
| City boundary, lakes, drains | OpenStreetMap (ODbL) |
| Ward boundaries | DataMeet Municipal Spatial Data |
| Ground elevation, 30 m | FABDEM (Copernicus GLO-30 with buildings and forests removed), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 |
| Built-up share | ESA WorldCover 2021 |
| Radar (2022 flood) | Copernicus Sentinel-1 GRD via Google Earth Engine |
| Land surface temperature | Landsat 8/9 Collection 2 Level 2 |

## Limitations

- The flood model shows susceptibility, not a hydrological simulation of water depth.
- Sentinel-1 struggles in dense urban areas and may not have imaged the flood peak; news-reported flood locations are used as a second validation set.
- Loan values in the sample portfolio are invented for demonstration.
